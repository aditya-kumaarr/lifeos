# LifeOS — User Interview Guide

**Purpose:** The field tool for Phase 0 Workstream 0.2 (user research). Use this exact script across all 15 interviews so synthesis is comparable.
**Owner of this doc:** Founder + designer.
**Date:** 23 May 2026.
**Related:** [Master Plan — Workstream 0.2](./LifeOS-Master-Plan.md), [v1 Product Spec](./LifeOS-v1-Product-Spec.md).

---

## Before each interview — the 60-second checklist

- Recording on (with explicit verbal consent at the start).
- Note-taker present, ideally the designer (you, the founder, focus on the conversation).
- Total time: 30 minutes hard stop. People drift after that.
- Have their LinkedIn or background open so warm-up is informed.
- Bring a notepad — they may want to show you their current notes.
- **Do not pitch the product.** This is the single most important rule. The moment you pitch, the conversation is contaminated. They will tell you what you want to hear. Just listen.

---

## Opening (2 minutes)

> "Thanks for taking the time. I'm doing research on how people capture and remember information from meetings and conversations — not pitching anything. I want to learn about your habits and frustrations. Mind if I record so I can listen back? Anything you say stays between us unless you say otherwise."

[Wait for consent. Start recording.]

> "Before we start — quick context on you. What do you do, and what does a typical week look like?"

[Listen. Don't take more than 2 minutes here. Use it to calibrate their context.]

---

## Section 1 — Their meeting reality (5 minutes)

These questions establish the baseline. Keep them open-ended.

1. **How many conversations or meetings would you say you have in a typical week — work, personal, calls with vendors, classes, anything where two or more people are talking and one of them might want to remember it later?**

2. **Walk me through your last meeting — not what was said, but what you did. What happened before it started? During? After?**
   - *Probe:* Did you prepare anything beforehand? Did you take notes? When? Did you do anything with those notes after?

3. **Of those meetings, how many do you genuinely care about remembering — versus the routine ones?**

4. **Are there meetings you wish you didn't have to remember at all, but you do? Like which?**

---

## Section 2 — Current tools and behaviour (8 minutes)

This section maps their actual workflow. Be specific. Avoid the trap of generic answers.

5. **What tools do you currently use, if any, for meeting notes? Walk me through the most recent example.**
   - *Probe:* Why that tool? Have you used others before? What made you switch — or stay?

6. **Can you show me your notes from your last meeting? Right now, on your screen.**
   - *This is the most important question in the guide.* What they actually have looks nothing like what they describe. Take screenshots if they let you.

7. **Have you ever tried any of these — Granola, Otter, Fireflies, Read.ai, Notion AI, ChatGPT for meeting notes? What happened?**
   - *Probe each one they've touched:* What did you like? What made you stop using it? What would have made you keep using it?

8. **When you need to find something from an old meeting — a number, a decision, who said what — what do you actually do?**
   - *Probe:* How often does that happen? How successful are you?

9. **Do you ever share meeting notes with someone else? How? In what format?**

---

## Section 3 — Pain points (10 minutes — most important section)

This is where the gold is. Go slow. Ask for stories, not opinions.

10. **Tell me about the last time you forgot something important from a meeting and it cost you something — a missed action, an awkward "wait what did we decide" moment, a redone task.**
    - *Probe:* What did you do? How did you feel? Has that happened more than once?

11. **What's the most frustrating part of how you capture meetings today?**
    - *Probe:* Is the friction in the moment of the meeting, after, or weeks later?

12. **If you could wave a magic wand and change one thing about how meetings get remembered — not the meetings themselves, just the remembering — what would you change?**

13. **Tell me about a time you wished you could ask "what did so-and-so say about X?" — but you couldn't find it.**

14. **Do you ever go back and look at notes from meetings that happened weeks or months ago? Why or why not?**
    - *Probe:* If yes, what are you usually looking for? If no, why bother taking notes at all?

15. **When you're in a meeting, what would you rather be doing than taking notes?**
    - *This question reveals the cost of note-taking better than asking about the cost directly.*

16. **Has anyone ever sent you AI-generated meeting notes — from Otter, Granola, anything? What was that experience like? Did you use them, ignore them, delete them?**

---

## Section 4 — Future state (3 minutes)

Brief, hypothetical, do not over-explain.

17. **If an app could capture every conversation you cared about — perfectly, automatically — and let you ask it questions later in plain English, what would you ask it?**
    - *Listen for: the type of question. "What did X say?" "What did I commit to?" "Have I discussed Y before?" The pattern reveals the use case.*

18. **What's the biggest thing such an app would have to do — or absolutely not do — for you to actually trust it?**

19. **Roughly, what would something like that be worth to you per month — if anything?**
    - *Probe:* Are you currently paying for any tool for this? How much?

---

## Section 5 — Wrap-up (2 minutes)

20. **Is there anything I should have asked you that I didn't?**
21. **Who else do you know who I should talk to? Especially anyone who does this differently from you.**
22. **Can I follow up in a few weeks if I have more questions?**

[Thank them. Stop the recording. Take 60 seconds before the next call to jot top 3 takeaways while fresh.]

---

## After each interview — the 5-minute synthesis

Do this *before* the next call. Memory decay is brutal.

- **Top three takeaways:** What surprised you? What confirmed or contradicted the spec?
- **One verbatim quote** that captures the interviewee's reality.
- **Tag the interview:** `validating` / `contradicting` / `pivoting` / `kill`.
- **Segment heatmap:** Which archetype did they fall into? How underserved did they feel on a 1–5 scale?
- **Note any names they suggested for follow-up interviews.**

Add to a shared running synthesis doc daily. By interview 10, patterns should be visible. If they're not, something is wrong with the script or the recruitment.

## Recruitment targets (15 total interviews)

To stress-test the universality claim in the spec, recruit across these archetypes:

- **Founders / startup teams** — 3 interviews. Source: personal network, accelerator alumni groups.
- **Sales reps (B2B SaaS / services)** — 3 interviews. Source: LinkedIn outreach, RevGenius, sales communities.
- **Students (college / postgrad)** — 3 interviews. Source: campus contacts, student communities, Discord.
- **Consultants / freelancers / agency owners** — 3 interviews. Source: LinkedIn, agency directories.
- **High-confidentiality professionals (doctors, therapists, lawyers)** — 3 interviews. Source: warm intros only (cold outreach has low yield here).

Mix Indian and non-Indian users — at least 8 Indian users to stress-test the Hindi-English angle.

## Red flags in a synthesis to watch for

- **Everyone says yes politely but no one is leaning in.** Politeness ≠ demand.
- **The pain is real but already solved adequately.** Granola users who like Granola is a problem for us, not a target.
- **Pain only appears in one segment.** That's not a kill — it's a signal to narrow v1.
- **The "magic wand" answers are all about *better existing apps*, not *new behaviour*.** If users only wish for incremental improvements, the super-app thesis is weak.
- **They can't show you any current notes when asked.** Either they don't take any (and don't miss it), or they're embarrassed. Either is a signal.
